## The real history behind it (all true)

- **2019 — the first plant on the Moon froze.** A cotton seedling in China's Chang'e-4 lander became the first plant ever to sprout on the Moon. It **did not survive its first lunar night**; the experiment was not designed to keep it alive through the cold.
- **2016 — an astronaut saved dying flowers.** On the space station, zinnias were dying of mould. NASA let **Scott Kelly care for them on his own judgement** instead of waiting for instructions from Earth. They **bloomed**.
- **Next — NASA plans to grow plants on the Moon.** The **LEAF** experiment is a sealed growth chamber for the lunar surface. It was picked for Artemis III; since NASA reshaped Artemis in 2026, it is being prepared for one of the first Artemis Moon landings (planned from 2028). One of its three plants is **Brassica rapa** (field mustard):
  - the classroom "Wisconsin Fast Plant", which goes from seed to flower in about 14 days;
  - the same species as Bangladesh's **Tori mustard (সরিষা)**.

## The story in five moments

### 1. Last Light
- **The base.** Aurora Station sits on the rim of Shackleton crater, near the Moon's south pole. Rim sites like this are among the best-lit places on the Moon, sunlit for most of the year. But tonight is its **longest night of the year**.
- **The cadets.** Four cadets from Last Light Academy are on their first night running the base alone:
  - **Kai** runs the power;
  - **Amara** makes the air;
  - **Mei** runs the garden;
  - **Rafi** guards the Safe Room.
- **Alo.** Outside, in a small sealed growth chamber like NASA's LEAF, sits **Alo** (আলো, "light"), a bud of field mustard. If Alo makes it to sunrise, it will be the first flower ever to bloom on the Moon.
- **Sunset.** The Sun slides behind the far mountains and the tall solar masts go dark. For the next **36 hours**, the base lives on whatever is in the battery.

### 2. The Drill
Commander Ferreira, at Mission Control on Earth, calls a storm drill: *"Practice building the shelter."* Rao, the friendly flight controller, cheers them on. Then Rafi gets a private message: *"Sunspot 2673 just fired a giant flare. Another big one may come."*

### 3. Earth-Set
From the south pole, Earth slowly sinks below the horizon. *"Earth-set in ten seconds,"* says Ferreira. *"From here, you're on your own."* The radio hisses, then goes silent.

**No Sun. No Earth.**

### 4. The Storm
- **The alarm.** Rafi's radiation counter starts ticking: the first, fastest particles of a giant solar storm, a replay of the real storm of **10 September 2017**. The screen flips: **THIS IS NOT A DRILL.**
- **Ninety seconds.** That's only enough time to pump a little water into the shelter walls, or to stack crates.
- **The rule.** The crew goes into the Safe Room first. That part is never a choice.
- **The catch.** The water that shields the crew is **the water Alo drinks**. In this scenario the storm's radiation is not what threatens Alo: frost and thirst are.

### 5. First Light
After a day and a half, a sliver of Sun climbs over the crater rim. The solar masts wake up. Light pours into Alo's chamber… and a tiny **yellow flower opens: the first flower ever to bloom on the Moon.**

Days later, Earth rises again and Ferreira's voice crackles back: *"You didn't survive the Moon. You ran it."*

### The reveal (in the debrief): what was real, what was computed, what was invented
The debrief sorts the night into three labelled kinds of evidence:
- **Observed (historical records):** the storm's timing is NASA DONKI's record of the 10 September 2017 event, and LRO's CRaTER instrument measured that storm at the Moon. On Mars, Curiosity measured radiation more than double earlier levels during the same event. LROC's polar map measured how often each spot near the pole is sunlit.
- **Computed (only once the team's processing is checked):** Aurora's horizon and the paths of the Sun and Earth, from LOLA terrain and JPL Horizons. Until that check is done, the 36-hour night and the Earth-set are labelled **training scenario**.
- **Simulated / fiction:** Aurora Station, the cadets, Alo, sun-drops and the other game units, the 2046 dates, and what the storm does to this base.

Proposed closing line (final wording after science review): *"Inspired by real lunar science and historical space-weather data. You played a fictional training mission built from verified observations, reconstructed geometry, and clearly marked educational simulations."* Drop "reconstructed geometry" if the computed night is not verified by release.

### How each ending closes
Chapter 1 finishes cleanly with every ending, so a first session never needs Chapter 2:
- **Golden Bloom / Bloom:** Alo opens at First Light. Tagging the photo plays N.'s old recording, and Mei finds Tara's bud indoors: *Alo needs a friend*. The season map shows Chapter 2 as part of the Season 1 roadmap.
- **Alo lost:** the crew is safe and Tara is still growing indoors. On the old computer, one locked recording waits, marked *for the first bloom*. The debrief names the decision to change, and the player can retry.
- **Mission stopped:** the flight rules stop the night before anyone is hurt. The debrief shows the cause and offers the last checkpoint.

## The long story: Season 1, *The First Moon Garden*

> **Turn one flower into the first garden on the Moon, in one real Moon year, and find out who left the seeds.**

The night above is only the beginning. Season 1 follows Alo's family for one Moon year, from one long winter night to the next (April 2046 to April 2047), in eight chapters. The game design for every chapter is in [`CAMPAIGN_SEASON1.md`](CAMPAIGN_SEASON1.md).

### Chapter 1 · Night One
Two weeks before the long night, Mei finds an old seed packet at the back of a drawer: two seeds, the word **আলো**, and one letter, **N.** She plants them both. Then comes the night you already know: no Sun, no Earth, a real storm… and at First Light, Alo blooms. At that exact moment, the base's old computer wakes up and plays a message recorded twelve years ago:
> *"If you're hearing this, Alo bloomed. Good. Now Alo needs a friend. — N."*

### Chapter 2 · Be the Bee
A mustard flower can't make seeds alone: it needs pollen from another plant. Alo lives outside; its partner, **Tara**, lives inside. There are no bees on the Moon, so the cadets become the bees. They carry pollen through the airlock on a "bee stick", just as astronaut Michael Foale did on the Mir space station in 1997. Mei remembers her grandmother, who pollinated apple trees by hand in China after the bees disappeared. That night, the ground trembles.

### Chapter 3 · Moonquake
A moonquake, like the one Apollo's seismometers felt near the south pole in 1973, shakes the base, and air starts leaking. Amara, who loves exact numbers, has to find the leak with numbers she can't trust. Meanwhile Alo's pods ripen, and Mei must stop watering so the seeds can dry. Alo's last petal falls. *"Forty-two seeds,"* says Amara. *"Alo made forty-two seeds."* The quake has cracked open a hatch under the base, marked **N.R. · EXPEDITION ZERO**. Below it lie a frozen garden, twelve years old, and a map to a crater that never sees the Sun.

### Chapter 4 · Into the Cold Trap
Five months later, Alo's seeds have become a garden of twelve. At midsummer, when the rim gets the most sunlight, Rafi leads a rover expedition down into a crater colder than −230 °C. The headlights find ice in the soil, and a frozen capsule: **Noor's seed vault**, twelve seeds kept safe for twelve years. Inside the lid:
> *"If you found this, you're ready. Ask Commander Ferreira about Expedition Zero."*

### Chapter 5 · Shooting Stars
While the Geminid meteor shower sparks tiny impact flashes across the dark plains, Commander Ferreira finally tells the truth. Twelve years ago, she was the youngest engineer of **Expedition Zero**. A storm broke their relay radio and they had to leave at sunset. Their gardener, **Dr. Noor Rahman**, stayed to the last minute to hide her seeds. Then Rao smiles: *"Noor is alive. She has read every one of your reports."*

### Chapter 6 · The Red Ring
On 12 January 2047 (a real eclipse date), Earth slides in front of the Sun. From the Moon, Earth becomes a black disc inside a glowing **red ring**: every sunrise and sunset on Earth at once, and one of them is the sunrise over Bangladesh. Kai, who has feared the dark since he was little, plans every minute of the eclipse, and isn't afraid. A message arrives from Earth: *"I watched a red ring from there once. — N."* Then a giant sunspot turns towards the Moon.

### Chapter 7 · The Great Storm
What if the great storm of July 2012, which missed Earth by nine days, had hit the Moon? Tonight, it does. The cadets protect the crew, the garden and the seeds through three waves of particles, and everything they have learned pays off. Ferreira steps back: *"Your call, cadets."* In the Safe Room, Noor's last log from Expedition Zero plays:
> *"We're leaving at last light. I'm calling the seeds Alo, light, so whoever finds them brings the light back."*

Then the storm burns out the radio, and Earth sets. Total silence.

### Chapter 8 · The Lighthouse
One Moon year after Night One, the longest night returns. While the base team survives it, Ferreira and two cadets drive to **Malapert Mountain**, whose summit can almost always see Earth, to fix the relay that Expedition Zero lost. As Earth sinks, the dish locks on, and a voice comes through:
> *"Hello, Aurora. This is Noor."*

From a rooftop in Bangladesh, under the Moon, she says: *"You did what I couldn't."* **Alo means light. So does Noor.** At First Light, the first garden on the Moon blooms yellow. Among the flowers grows **cotton**, the plant that froze on the Moon in 2019, alive at sunrise. Ferreira: *"You didn't survive the Moon. You grew it."*

*After the credits:* *"There's one drawer in the vault you haven't opened."* Its label: **MARS**.

**From Last Light to First Light.** The game begins at sunset, the "last light" Noor saw as she left the Moon, and ends with the first Moon garden's sunrise.
