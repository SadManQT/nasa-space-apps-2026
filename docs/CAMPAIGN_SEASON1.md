### 1.2 What people of every age enjoy

| Finding | Source | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Age changes what gamers like more than gender does. The wish for **competition** falls the most with age; **strategy** stays the same at every age | Quantic Foundry (more than 350,000 gamers surveyed) | A new toy every chapter for younger players; strategy (upgrades) for everyone; no competitive mode for kids |
| From age 36 on, the top motivations include **Fantasy** (a world to step into) and **Completion** (collecting everything) | Quantic Foundry | A rich story world and a full collection |
| 205 million Americans aged 5 to 90 play games; the average player is 36 | ESA *Essential Facts* 2025 | Design for whole families, not just kids |
| Why people play: joy (84%), mental stimulation and stress relief (81%), bringing people together (76%), learning (70%) | ESA 2025 | Fun first, co-op, real science |

| Games pull us in when they meet three needs: **competence** (I'm getting good), **autonomy** (my choices matter) and **relatedness** (I'm with people I care about) | Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006 | Stars and mastery; upgrade choices; co-op and characters to love |

### 1.3 What makes a story hard to put down

| Technique | Where it comes from | In Season 1 |
|---|---|---|
| **An open question.** People remember unfinished things better than finished ones (the Zeigarnik effect, 1927) | TV cliffhangers; Telltale episodes | Every chapter ends on a question, and one mystery runs through the season |
| **Failure moves the story** | Hades | Some logs and scenes appear only after a loss |
| **Something new every chapter** | It Takes Two | 8 chapters, 8 new toys |
| **Knowledge is the reward** | Outer Wilds | Logs and data cards hold real clues you use later |
| **Someone small to care for** | Cozy games and virtual pets (without the guilt) | Alo and Alo's family |
| **Talk to win** | Spaceteam; Keep Talking | Secrets, and split teams on the radio |
| **A big promise from minute one** | "Will they make it home?" | "Turn one flower into a garden", shown on the map from the start |

### 1.4 What we refuse to do

Research on games for young people warns against **"dark patterns"**:
- loot boxes and virtual currencies;
- "play by appointment" timers and fear of missing out;
- paywalls and battle passes.

A systematic review links paid random rewards with problem gambling among young people, and even games for under-5s use these tricks. So our "pull" comes **only** from story, care, mastery and friends. The rules are in §13.

## 2. The season at a glance

**The goal:** one flower → the first garden on the Moon. The Season map shows the garden growing, chapter by chapter.

**Four questions keep you playing:**
1. Can Alo's family grow into a garden? *(care)*
2. Who is "N.", who left the seeds? *(mystery)*
3. What happened to the first crew here? *(mystery)*
4. Can the cadets end the silence when Earth sets? *(goal)*

### A real Moon year

The Moon's south pole has tiny seasons. The Sun climbs only about 1.5° above or below the horizon there, but that is enough to make long winter nights and long summer days. One pole "year" lasts about 350 days. Season 1 runs from one midwinter to the next:

| When | Date (computed) | Pole season |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 1 | ~25 April 2046 | ❄️ Midwinter: the longest nights |
| Chapter 4 | ~19 October 2046 | ☀️ Midsummer: the most sunlight |
| Chapter 8 | ~10 April 2047 | ❄️ Midwinter again |

*We computed these dates from the Moon's orbit with the PyEphem library. At the hackathon we confirm them with NASA JPL Horizons and the real horizon from LOLA. Tag [E].*

The game is set in the 2040s, so **today's players could be the real crew by then**.

### How a chapter works (like a TV episode, 20–45 minutes)

1. **Cold open** (1 min): a short animated scene.
2. **Mission** (15–30 min): the Chapter 1 controls you already know, plus **one new toy**.
3. **Debrief: "It was real!"** (3 min): stars, NASA's team skills, and the real data behind the chapter.
4. **Cliffhanger** (30 s): a question for next time.
5. **Upgrade choice:** pick one of two real NASA technologies. Your base changes for the rest of the season.
6. **Station Days** (optional, 5–10 min): a calm hub to talk to the crew, tend the garden, find logs and play side stories.

## 3. The secret backstory (spoilers!)

Players uncover this one piece per chapter.

- **2034, twelve years before Season 1:** the first crew, **Expedition Zero**, built a small hut on Shackleton's rim.
- Their gardener was **Dr. Noor Rahman**, a plant scientist from northern Bangladesh, where the winter fields turn yellow with mustard.
- Their youngest engineer was **Ferreira**, today's Commander.
- A huge solar storm broke the crew's only relay radio. Earth set. They had to leave in a hurry, **at sunset: the last light**.
- Before leaving, Noor:
  1. hid her seed collection in the coldest place she knew, a small crater that never sees the Sun (colder than −230 °C: a natural freezer);
  2. left two seeds in the garden drawer, labelled **আলো (Alo, "light")** and **তারা (Tara, "star")**, signed with one letter: **N.**;
  3. recorded a message, set to play "when the first flower opens".
- Her own garden froze, just like the real cotton sprout on the Moon in 2019.
- Back on Earth, Noor founded **Last Light Academy**, named after the last sunset she saw on the Moon. Its purpose: train young crews to finish what she started.
- Ferreira promised not to tell the cadets: *"Seeds you find mean more than seeds you're given."*
- **The hidden clue:** in Bangla, *Alo* means light. *Noor* means light too (in Arabic, and in Bangla). She named the seed after herself.

**One reveal per chapter:**

| Ch | What the players learn |
|---|---|
| 1 | A 12-year-old message: "If you're hearing this, Alo bloomed." Who is N.? |
| 2 | In a log, N. remembers her grandmother's yellow mustard fields |
| 3 | A hatch under the base: "N.R. · EXPEDITION ZERO", and her frozen first garden |
| 4 | Her seed vault, and a note: "Ask Commander Ferreira." |
| 5 | Ferreira tells the truth. N. is **Noor**, and she is alive |
| 6 | During the eclipse, a text from Earth: "I watched a red ring from there once. — N." |
| 7 | Noor's last log from Expedition Zero: why the seeds are called Alo, and why the Academy is called Last Light |
| 8 | Noor herself, live from Bangladesh |

## 4. The eight chapters

### Chapter 1 · Night One 🌙
*~April 2046, midwinter · the whole crew · 25–30 min · the full rules are in `GAME_PLAN_FINAL.md`*

- **New opening:** two weeks before the night, Mei finds an old seed packet at the back of the garden drawer. Inside: two seeds, the word **আলো**, and one letter, **N.** She plants one outside in the sealed chamber (Alo) and one inside under the lamp, "just in case".
- **The mission:** the v7 night, unchanged. The Sun sets for about 36 hours, Earth sets, a real storm hits (a replay of 10 September 2017), and at First Light Alo blooms.
- **New ending (the hook):** as Alo opens, the base's old computer wakes up. A message recorded 12 years ago, set to play "when the first flower opens":
  > *"If you're hearing this, Alo bloomed. Good. Now Alo needs a friend. — N."*

  Mei turns the packet over: **"Alo can't make seeds alone."** In the indoor pot, the second seed has a bud. Its label: **তারা, Tara, "star".**
- **Real science:** Fast Plants can't pollinate themselves. They need pollen from another plant.
- **Upgrade choice:** ☀️ Taller mast or 🔋 Fuel-cell battery (§9).

### Chapter 2 · Be the Bee 🐝
*Late April 2046 · Mei's chapter · 20–25 min*

- **Cold open:** Mei remembers her grandmother's apple orchard in Maoxian, China. The bees there disappeared, so farmers pollinated every flower by hand, with feathers on sticks. *"When the bees can't come, we become the bees."*
- **Real history:** in 1997, astronaut Michael Foale grew *Brassica rapa* on the Mir space station. He pollinated the flowers with a **bee stick** (a dried bee glued to a stick). They made the first seeds produced in space that grew into new plants.
- **The mission (7 days of flowering):** Alo is outside; Tara is inside. To make seeds, someone must carry pollen through the airlock.
- **New toy: bee runs.**
  - Swipe the bee stick across Alo's flowers, carry the yellow pollen through the airlock and dust Tara's flowers, then go back the other way.
  - Each airlock trip costs air and a sun-drop.
  - Rafi checks the space weather before every walk outside.
  - Fast Plants want light 24 hours a day, so Mei's lamp eats power.
- **The colour guess:** Alo has a purple stem; Tara's is green. *What colour will their babies' stems be?* Everyone votes. The answer arrives in Chapter 4.
- **"It was real!":** Foale's bee stick on Mir; the classroom Fast Plants method; why Fast Plants need a partner.
- **Cliffhanger:** that night the dome hums. The seismometer spikes. Rafi: *"Was that… a moonquake?"*
- **Upgrade choice:** 💡 Red-blue LEDs or 🧣 Insulation blankets.
- **Learn:** how plant structures and pollinators affect reproduction (NGSS MS-LS1-4); why offspring vary (MS-LS3-2).

### Chapter 3 · Moonquake 〰️
*May 2046 · Amara's chapter · 25 min*

- **Cold open:** the real sound of a moonquake (an Apollo seismogram turned into sound). Then the base starts to shake.
- **Real history:**
  - Apollo's seismometers (1969–1977) recorded 28 shallow moonquakes, up to about magnitude 5.
  - One of the strongest, on 13 March 1973, came from the south-pole region.
  - The Moon is slowly shrinking as it cools (by about 50 m), and its brittle crust cracks into faults. LRO found young faults near the south pole (Watters et al. 2024).
- **The mission:** after the quake, air is leaking somewhere.
- **New toy: the leak hunt.**
  - Six places could be leaking, and the gauges are noisy.
  - Each shift you can check only one or two places.
  - Aftershocks can open a second leak.
  - Amara, who loves exact numbers, must act on *uncertain* numbers: best guess, check, update.
- **Alo's family:** Alo's seed pods are ripening. Mei must **stop watering** at the right moment, so the seeds dry and ripen. It's a real Fast Plants step, and the hardest thing Mei has ever done.
- **The big emotional moment: Alo's Gift.** Alo is an annual plant: after making seeds, it fades. Its last petal falls.
  - Amara counts the pods: *"Forty-two seeds. Alo made forty-two seeds."*
  - Mei: *"Then every first flower in this garden will be called Alo."*
- **"It was real!":** Apollo seismograms (NASA PDS archive), the 1973 south-pole quake, the shrinking Moon.
- **Cliffhanger:** the quake cracked open a hatch under the storeroom: **"N.R. · EXPEDITION ZERO"**. Below it: a frozen garden, 12 years old, and a hand-drawn map to a small crater that never sees the Sun: *"Coldest place. Safest place. — N."*
- **Upgrade choice:** 🪨 Moon-dirt shelter or 🔩 Flexible seals.
- **Learn:** waves carry energy, and bigger waves carry more (MS-PS4-1); how surfaces change over time (MS-ESS2-2, applied to the Moon); plant life cycles.

### Chapter 4 · Into the Cold Trap 🧊
*October 2046, midsummer · Rafi's chapter · 30–35 min*

- **Cold open: five months later.**
  - Alo's seeds sprout. Was your colour guess right?
  - Over four generations, the garden grows to 12 plants.
  - Ferreira approves an expedition: *"We go at midsummer, when the rim gets the most sunlight to charge the rover."* (True: at midsummer the pole gets the most sun.)
- **Rafi's moment:** the worrier becomes the Expedition Safety Lead. His worries become the checklist.
- **New toy: the split team.** Two cadets drive the rover; two run the base. The base team sees the slope map; the rover team sees only what the headlights show. They must talk by radio, like *Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes*.
  - The rover can't climb slopes steeper than 15°, like NASA's VIPER rover.
  - In the dark crater it's colder than −230 °C, so the heater eats battery.
  - Inside the crater there's no straight line to the base, so the radio fails. Drop small radio beacons on the way in. *(This sets up Chapter 8.)*
- **Discoveries:**
  - **ice mixed into the soil**, which the base can use. Real: in 2009 NASA's LCROSS found about 5.6% water in Cabeus crater's soil;
  - **Noor's seed vault**, a frozen capsule with 12 seeds. Real: in 2024, Smithsonian scientists proposed storing frozen samples in these craters, because they stay cold enough without electricity.
- **"It was real!":** LOLA slope maps; ShadowCam (a NASA camera on Korea's Danuri orbiter that sees inside always-dark craters); LCROSS; Diviner temperatures; VIPER.
- **Cliffhanger:** inside the vault lid: *"If you found this, you're ready. Ask Commander Ferreira about Expedition Zero."* Back at base, Ferreira's channel goes quiet. Rao: *"She… needs a minute."*
- **Upgrade choice:** 🧊 Ice harvester or 🔦 Rover lights and heater.
- **Learn:** heat, cold and states of matter (MS-PS1-4); why resources end up where they are (MS-ESS3-1, applied to the Moon).

### Chapter 5 · Shooting Stars 🌠
*~14 December 2046, the Geminid meteor shower · Ferreira's chapter · 25 min*

- **Cold open: Ferreira tells the truth.** Expedition Zero, the storm, the broken relay, leaving at sunset.
  > *"Noor stayed to the last minute to hide the seeds. I was the engineer who couldn't fix the radio. We left her garden to freeze. I left it."*
- **Then Rao's surprise:** *"Noor is alive. She's read every one of your mission reports. She asked about Alo by name."*
- **The mission:** the Geminid meteor shower. Earth and the Moon pass through the same stream of space dust.
- **New toy: flash watch and dust patrol.**
  - Spot impact flashes on the dark plains and count them, like NASA's real team. A relaxed setting needs no fast reflexes.
  - Tiny hits spray dust on the solar panels (less power) and pepper the dome with micro-leaks: clean and patch.
- **Fun:** Rao's terrible meteor jokes. *"Why was the meteor so sad? It had a crater-ble day."*
- **"It was real!":**
  - NASA's Marshall Space Flight Center has watched the Moon for impact flashes since 2006: hundreds recorded, dozens in a single Geminid night.
  - LRO found 222 new craters made during its mission (Speyerer et al. 2016).
  - Apollo 17's Harrison Schmitt got "lunar hay fever" from Moon dust.
  - NASA's Electrodynamic Dust Shield cleaned dust off surfaces on the Moon in March 2025.
- **Cliffhanger (a promise):** Ferreira: *"In four weeks, Earth will swallow the Sun. This time, I want you to see it."*
- **Upgrade choice:** ⚡ Dust Shield or 🛡️ Whipple bumper.
- **Learn:** kinetic energy (speed matters more than size) (MS-PS3-1); comparing shield designs (MS-ETS1-2).

### Chapter 6 · The Red Ring 🔴
*12 January 2047, a real total lunar eclipse · Kai's chapter · 25 min*

- **Cold open:** Kai remembers a blackout during a storm when he was small. Ever since, he has hated switching anything off.
- **Real event:** on 12 January 2047 there is a total lunar eclipse (greatest at 01:25 UT, in Fred Espenak's eclipse catalog). Seen from the Moon, it's the opposite: **Earth covers the Sun**.
  - **Our check:** from the south pole, the Sun and Earth sit right on the horizon. Earth hides the Sun completely for about **2 h 40 min** (about 00:00–02:42 UT), and partly for about 4 h 45 min. *[E]*
  - Earth's air glows as a **red ring**: every sunrise and sunset on Earth, all at once.
  - **At 00:43 UT, sunrise reaches Dhaka.** So part of that red ring is a Bangladeshi sunrise.
- **New toy: the countdown planner.**
  - Unlike a storm, an eclipse is known to the minute, decades ahead.
  - Plan a timeline: charge, power down, keep the garden warm, and be at the window with the camera at the right moment.
  - It's short but sudden: the masts go dark in minutes.
- **Photo mode:** take a picture of the red ring, a postcard for your album.
- **Fun:**
  - An eclipse picnic in the dark.
  - Earth is up (it's right in front of the Sun!), so families call from home. Kai's family calls during the darkest minute, and he isn't afraid.
- **Kai's line:** *"The dark is okay. If you know when it's coming, you can get ready."*
- **Noor's message:** *"I watched a red ring from there once. Beautiful, isn't it? — N."*
- **"It was real!":** the eclipse catalog; LRO's Diviner watches the ground cool fast during eclipses; why the ring is red (for the same reason sunsets are red).
- **Cliffhanger:** Rafi's space-weather feed flags a giant sunspot group turning towards the Moon. Ferreira, quietly: *"That's the biggest one I've seen since Expedition Zero."*
- **Upgrade choice:** 📡 Storm early warning or 📦 Bigger Safe Room.
- **Learn:** the Sun–Earth–Moon system and eclipses (MS-ESS1-1); light that is bent and filtered (MS-PS4-2).

### Chapter 7 · The Great Storm ⚡
*February 2047 · Rafi and Ferreira · 35–40 min · the finale, part one*

- **Cold open (all true):**
  > *"On 23 July 2012, one of the fastest solar storms ever measured blasted past NASA's STEREO-A spacecraft. Nine days earlier, it would have hit Earth. In August 1972, between Apollo 16 and Apollo 17, a huge storm swept past the Moon. Nobody was there. Tonight, somebody is."*
- **Honest label:** this chapter is a **"what if"**. What if the 2012 storm had hit the Moon? It replays STEREO-A's real measurements at our base.
- **New toy: the three-wave storm.** First the fastest particles, then the shock wave, then a long tail. Protect three things at once:
  1. **the crew** (the Safe Room);
  2. **the flowering garden** (frost and thirst, as in Chapter 1);
  3. **the seeds.** Dry seeds are tough: NASA's tomato seeds spent almost six years in orbit, then 3.3 million students grew them. Growing plants are not as tough. *Seeds first.*
- **Every character's arc pays off:**
  - Kai planned the power days ahead.
  - Amara decides on her best estimate, then checks.
  - Mei lets the old plants go to save their seeds.
  - Rafi runs the Safe Room calmly: *"Worry is just a plan you haven't written down yet."*
  - Ferreira steps back: *"Your call, cadets."* (Just as NASA let Scott Kelly decide how to save his zinnias.)
- **Your upgrades matter:** every choice since Chapter 1 changes your options tonight.
- **Noor's last log from Expedition Zero** plays in the Safe Room:
  > *"We're leaving at last light. I'm calling the seeds Alo, light, so whoever finds them brings the light back."*
- **The twist:** the storm's peak fries the base's radio. Earth sets. Total silence, and no relay. The only place that can still see Earth is the old relay on **Malapert Mountain**, dead since Expedition Zero.
- **"It was real!":** STEREO-A and DONKI (2012); the August 1972 storm; why 20 g/cm² of water makes a storm shelter.
- **Cliffhanger:** Ferreira: *"Twelve years ago, I couldn't fix that relay. Will you help me finish it?"*
- **Upgrade choice:** 📻 Relay parts kit or 🛞 Long-range rover battery.
- **Learn:** forecasting natural hazards and protecting against them (MS-ESS3-2); comparing designs using test data (MS-ETS1-3).

### Chapter 8 · The Lighthouse 🗼
*~April 2047, midwinter again · everyone · 35–45 min · the finale*

- **Cold open:** one Moon year after Night One, the longest night returns. This time the garden has dozens of plants. And Earth is about to set again.
- **New toy: two teams, one night.**
  - **Trek team** (Ferreira and two cadets): drive about 120 km to **Malapert Mountain**, about 5 km high. Its summit can see Earth almost all the time. At the top, aim the dish at Earth's real position in the sky (an aiming puzzle with two dials).
  - **Base team:** survive the long night again, as in Chapter 1, but now with your upgrades, a bigger garden and everything you've learned. What was hard in Chapter 1 now feels doable. *Look how far you've come.*
- **The climax:** as Earth sinks at the base, the dish locks on with seconds to spare. The radio crackles, and it isn't Mission Control:
  > *"Hello, Aurora. This is Noor."*

  Dr. Noor Rahman, on a rooftop in Bangladesh, the Moon above her. *"Twelve years I waited for this signal. You did what I couldn't."*
  The cadets realise: **Alo means light. So does Noor.**
- **First Light:** the Sun rises, and **the first garden on the Moon** blooms: a carpet of yellow mustard (Alo's great-great-grandchildren) with the vault's plants. Among them is a **cotton plant**, the species that froze on the Moon in 2019, alive at sunrise.
- **Ferreira:** *"You didn't survive the Moon. You grew it."*
- **After the credits:** Noor: *"One more thing. There's a drawer in the vault you haven't opened."* The label says **MARS**. *Season 2 is coming.*
- **"It was real!":** Malapert's view of Earth (proposed for relays); NASA's LunaNet plan for a Moon communications network; Earth's real position from JPL Horizons.
- **After the finale:** Moon Garden mode, Live Sky and Storm Watch unlock (§11).
- **Learn:** digital signals and line of sight (MS-PS4-3); why Earth sets at the pole but not at Malapert's summit (MS-ESS1-1).

**The title, explained at last:** the game starts at **Last Light** (the sunset, and the sunset Noor saw as she left) and ends at **First Light** (the sunrise over the first Moon garden).

## 6. Alo's family: real plant science

Alo is a Wisconsin Fast Plant (*Brassica rapa*), the species NASA's LEAF experiment is being built to grow on the Moon. Its real life cycle drives the story:

| Day | The real Fast Plant | In the season |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | The seed is sown | Two weeks before Night One |
| ~14 | Flowers open | Alo blooms at First Light (Ch 1) |
| 14–17 | Pollinate with a bee stick, using a second plant | Be the Bee (Ch 2) |
| ~35 | Pods are full; stop watering so they dry | Moonquake (Ch 3) |
| ~40 | Harvest the seeds; the plant's life ends | Alo's Gift (Ch 3) |
| A new seed | The next generation starts | Every chapter after that |

- **One generation takes about 40 days**, so one Moon year holds **about nine generations**. The garden grows from one flower to a field.
- **Real genetics:** one gene decides whether a stem is purple or green, and purple wins. Players guess, then see the answer in Alo's babies (Ch 4) and grandchildren.
- **Alo's Family Tree:** a collectible tree showing every generation and its stem colour. Players **name** the first sprout of each generation.

## 10. Failing forward, and modes for every age

**A loss never blocks the story for long:**
1. Retry from the start of the last shift, not from the beginning of the chapter.
2. A loss unlocks a "what went wrong" replay and one of **Noor's failure logs** (*"My first garden froze too. Here's what I learned…"*).
3. After two losses, Rao offers a hint card, and the gentle Family Mode settings are always one tap away (we never call them "Easy").

## 13. Healthy by design

We want players to *want* to come back, never to *feel they have to*.

1. **No ads, no purchases, no loot boxes,** no paid shortcuts and no virtual currency.
2. **No daily rewards, streaks or "come back or lose it" timers.** Alo never suffers while you're away: the garden only changes while you play.
3. **No notifications.** Storm Watch is opt-in and appears only inside the game.
4. **Natural stopping points.** Every chapter ends with a calm "Good night, Alo" screen. Chapters fit one class period or one evening.
5. **Collectibles are earned,** never random or paid. The album shows exactly what's missing and where to look.
6. **Kind failure.** Losing gives story and hints, never shame.
7. **Privacy.** No accounts, no personal data, and it works offline. Class Mode stores nothing about students.
8. **Honest science.** Every number carries a tag ([M] measured, [E] estimate, [G] gameplay, [F] fiction), as in `SCIENCE_AND_DATA.md`.
