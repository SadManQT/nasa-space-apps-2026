## B. Bangladesh submission rules

Source: [Bangladesh Participants Guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1mSOxplyht5kii8GcCnFaGA-vMHIAVypjl4N6PobnaYQ/edit) (linked from the Dhaka event page).

**Two registrations**
> "Bangladesh runs a national pre-registration alongside the global NASA registration. They are separate systems and you need both."
> "Pre-registering in Bangladesh does not register you with NASA."

**The three videos**

| Video | Max | Must contain | Where | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 · Prescreening ("240 Seconds of Glory") | 240 s | Team name; every team member, listed by name; problem and challenge statement; solution approach — **concept only** | Upload to YouTube, paste the link into the Bangladesh form/portal | 7 Oct, 11:59 PM (portal) — page and guide say 1 Oct |
| 2 · Local judging | 240 s | All of the above, plus the solution with **a demo of it working**, and **say your NASA data usage out loud** | YouTube link into the form, **and** upload the file to the Google Drive folder provided | 13 Nov, 6:30 PM (confirm) |
| 3 · Global judging ("30 Seconds of Glory") | 30 s | All of the above, plus all Space Apps documentation completed | YouTube or Vimeo; paste the link into the form **and** the NASA project page | 14 Nov, 12:00 PM |

**Hard rules for the 240-second videos (quoted)**
- "Under 240 seconds. Over-length videos get cut off, not excused."
- "Name every team member — both videos, every time."
- "Credit any third-party asset, template, or generated content you used."
- "Video 1 secures your seat, and it can be re-cut right up to the hackathon."

**Where to submit now:** log in at [nasaspaceappsbd.com/participant/login](https://www.nasaspaceappsbd.com/participant/login). Required fields:
- "Kindly Upload Your 240s Video \*" (paste a link)
- "Kindly Upload Project Source Code Link (Git/Drive) \*"
- Project Name; Project Description
- "What is your NASA Space Apps Challenge category from the 2026 site?"
- "Please share here the URL of the team you created on NASA's global website." (example: `https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/find-a-team/team-voyagers/?tab=details`)
- "Which NASA Data Source Are You Using?"

Registration fields: team name; leader name, mobile, email; region (Dhaka, Rajshahi, Rangpur, Sylhet, Mymensingh, Khulna, Chittagong, Barisal, Cumilla); members (name, email, mobile), up to 6; team group photo; a question about female team members.

No rule was found on file naming, video format/resolution, or language for videos 1 and 2.

**Important:** the global FAQ says *"Teams are not allowed to begin working on the challenges prior to the hackathon."* The guide says Video 1 is "concept only", built "from the challenge summaries released on 17 September". Keep everything before 13 Nov at concept level.

## E. AI, copyright, minors, logos

**Terms** ([spaceappschallenge.org/legal](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/), updated 7 Aug 2026)
- No explicit AI clause.
- Solutions are not posted if they include material "for which you do not hold the copyright or other intellectual property rights."
- Original content must be OSI open source; if unspecified, NASA distributes it under Apache 2.0.
- "All resources, even open source resources, must be cited."
- Videos: you warrant "the video was produced and/or edited by you… (your original work)" and that its contents, including audio, "do not infringe the copyright… of any third party."
- **Minors:** "Recognizable names, voices, and likenesses of persons under the age of 18 should not be included in any video submissions." Adults shown in a video must give written consent.

**Bangladesh guide on AI**
> "Can I use AI-generated content? Yes, with conditions: you need a valid reason for using it, you must explain your prompts, and you must show where your own creative work went. Unexplained generated content is not acceptable, and any tool used to generate content must be named in your description."

- Use of AI field: "Every AI tool in your solution, named — with the prompts and reasoning behind them, and the data the AI worked on."
- Stock content: "add a disclaimer, credit the source, and give a valid reason." No copyrighted material (code, images, audio, footage, datasets, music) without explicit consent.
- Logos: no participant rule on the NASA insignia was found. Do not use the NASA "meatball" or "worm" logos, and never suggest NASA endorsement.

## F. Our challenge: "Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer" (confirmed for 2026)

[Challenge page](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/2026/challenges/build-a-junior-astronaut-mission-trainer/) · published 17 Sep 2026 · levels: Intermediate; Beginner/Youth · tags: Games, Planets & Moons, Software, Space Exploration, Sun.

> "Space-themed STEM content often oversimplifies the engineering trade-offs that define a real mission or presents them at a level too complex to hold a young learner's attention. Few tools make those trade-offs both tangible and fun. Your challenge is to design and build an interactive game or app that lets students run a lunar or Martian outpost, balancing competing demands like life support, radiation shielding, power, and food production, so they experience firsthand the decisions that determine whether a mission fails or succeeds."

The Resources tab is empty for now; the full statement with NASA data sets is published on **28 October**. Related 2026 challenges to avoid mixing up: "Space Mission Design Game", "Create Health Monitoring Software for Astronauts on Space Missions".
