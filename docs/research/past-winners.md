# Past NASA Space Apps global winners (2019–2025), and what they teach us

Researched on 6 October 2026 for Team The Last BrainCell.

**How the list was checked**
- **2019–2022:** taken from the archived award pages (`20XX.spaceappschallenge.org/awards/`). Each winner's project page was opened.
- **2023–2025:** taken from NASA's official announcements. Those give countries but rarely cities. The project pages on the main site could not be opened during research, so those links are unverified.

---

## What the winners teach us (10 takeaways)

1. **A kids' learning game wins almost every year.** Examples:
   - 2019: "Plan-It"
   - 2020: "Mars Calling" and "Survivors"
   - 2021: Space Travelers
   - 2022: "Diamond In The Sky"
   - 2023: the Eclipse tool and TeamVoyagers
   - 2024: "Eco-Metropolis" and TerraTales
   - 2025: HerCode Space and Zumorroda-X
2. **Games win the "story" awards.** They take Best Storytelling, Most Inspirational, Art & Technology and Best Mission Concept. Best Use of Data and Best Use of Science usually go to machine-learning or data tools. **Our best targets are Best Storytelling and Most Inspirational. Best Use of Data is a stretch, and only if the real-data replay is strong.**
3. **Bangladesh has won with kids' games before.** Team Diamonds (Cumilla) won Most Inspirational in 2022 with a game for ages 10–12. TeamVoyagers (Rajshahi) won Best Storytelling in 2023 with a web game.
4. **A crew-survival game for kids has already won.** In 2021, Space Travelers (Ecuador) won Best Storytelling for a 10–14 game built on the challenge "The Trail to Mars: Can You Keep Your Crew Alive". **"Keep the crew alive" alone is not new.** We must be clearly different: cooperative play with split information, and replayed real events.
5. **A communication limit can be the whole game.** In 2020, "Mars Calling", an escape room about Earth–Mars communication, won Best Mission Concept. Our Earth-set idea belongs in that family, but uses real Moon geometry.
6. **Real NASA data sits at the core of the winning games, not on the side.** "Eco-Metropolis" is driven by real environmental data. The moonquake winners animate Apollo seismic records.
7. **Moon-data projects keep winning.** "Make a Moonquake Map!" produced three winners (2022 Selene, 2022 Brute Force, 2023 LunarTech Ensemble), plus Spacebee in 2023. **Real lunar data, made visible, impresses NASA judges.**
8. **Space weather for children is already covered.** HerCode Space won Best Storytelling in 2025, and Bangladesh's 2025 finalist (CUET, ExoVisionaries) also built space-weather education for children. **Our solar storm cannot be the only hook.**
9. **Winning descriptions name an exact audience** ("kids 10–14", "kids 10–12") and **one clear promise**.
10. **Judging is video-first.** Locally and globally, judges mostly see the videos. A story that a global audience can understand and remember in 240 seconds (or 30 seconds) is part of the product.
