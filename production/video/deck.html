The problem

Real missions are trade-offs.
Most space lessons hide them.

Space-themed STEM content often oversimplifies the engineering trade-offs that define a real mission or presents them at a level too complex to hold a young learner's attention. Few tools make those trade-offs both tangible and fun. Challenge statement, Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer (NASA Space Apps 2026)

A trade-off is only felt when it costs something you care about. So we gave the player one small, living thing to protect: Alo, a mustard plant, through one lunar night.

The challenge · theme "The Next Frontier"

Build a Junior Astronaut Mission Trainer

"Your challenge is to design and build an interactive game or app that lets students run a lunar or Martian outpost, balancing competing demands like life support, radiation shielding, power, and food production, so they experience firsthand the decisions that determine whether a mission fails or succeeds."

Life support

An air tank of 10 units. Four crew breathe 1 unit per 7.2-hour shift (NASA BVAD). Air at 0 stops the mission.

Radiation shielding

A water wall of up to 6 units (20 g/cm² of water) plus supply crates. Storm dose must stay at 3 or less.

Power

A battery of 20 sun-drops (≈ 10 kWh each). Daylight adds 8 per shift; the 36-hour night adds none.

Food production

The garden, Alo's health and 12 crew-days of rations. Light and warmth cost power; water is shared with the shield.

Experience it firsthand: plan the shift → Try it shows the real forecast → Lock in → the station lives with it → four endings, a decision replay and a science debrief.

Impact and next steps

A free mission trainer any classroom can run

Who it reaches

  • Learners aged 10 to 15, in classrooms and at home
  • Runs offline on Windows and macOS, no account, no internet
  • Free and open source (Apache-2.0): schools can copy, translate and adapt it
  • One Chapter 1 night is designed to take about 20 minutes: it fits in a lesson
  • From Bangladesh to the Moon: Alo is Brassica rapa, the species of Bangladesh's mustard and of the Fast Plants grown in classrooms worldwide

Next steps

  • Four-player Class mode: shared decisions, private role clues on one machine (in progress, M1-37)
  • Interactive puzzle panels for Chapters 2–8 (M3-2P to M3-8P)
  • The learner playtest, then fixes from what players show us (M1-22, M1-24)
  • The release build for judging on 12 November (M3-93)
  • Two registered sources still to process: the LROC illumination map and the 2017 CRaTER storm curve
