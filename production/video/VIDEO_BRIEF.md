## 2. How the judges score (Bangladesh sheet)

Impact 20 · Creativity 20 · Validity 20 · Relevance 20 · Presentation 20 · Teamwork 5 · User experience 5 · NASA data 5 · three yes/no points. Teams with a woman member get +5%; we do not, so Impact, Creativity and Presentation have to carry it.

## 3. What the judges told teams (mentor boot camp, 4 Nov, in Bangla)

Source: the NASA Space Apps Bangladesh boot camp video, <https://www.facebook.com/share/v/1Es1GLXYro/>. Speakers: mentors Abdullah Al Araf and Rahat Uddin. Our transcript is kept offline in `reference_local/` (not in the public repository).

- **The problem comes first.** Most teams rush it. Tell it as a story about one person, back it with data, and show it is real and matches the challenge.
- **Creativity means the idea is new.** NASA wants a concept that could solve the challenge, not a long feature list.
- **Show your own prototype working.** No clips from anywhere else in the solution part. Stock footage is allowed only in the problem part.
- **Impact needs numbers:** how many people benefit in 5 or 20 years, and how the project grows.
- **NASA data must be visible to the user.** Put a small source note in a corner each time a fact is spoken. End with a 10-second slide listing every dataset with its website, plus the AI tools.
- **The first 10 to 13 seconds decide** whether a judge stays interested.
- **A recorded slideshow scores lower.** Each member presenting a part counts for teamwork.
- **Watch past winners' videos** and be newer and better.
- **30-second video:** intro, why, solution, future impact.

## 5. Our one-line idea

> Mission Control in a classroom: four kids, one offline laptop, each holding one piece of the truth, flying a real Moon night replayed from NASA records.

What only we have (say these, not the feature count):
1. Four players on one offline laptop with split information (Class mode).
2. Real history replayed: the 10 September 2017 storm and its real 70-minute warning.
3. Every number labelled Observed, Computed or Training assumption.
4. A living thing to protect: Alo, a Bangladeshi mustard plant.

## 6. Facts we may say, with sources

Game and NASA facts (every one is in the bundled data, see [docs/SCIENCE_AND_DATA.md](../../docs/SCIENCE_AND_DATA.md)):
- A cotton seed sprouted on the Moon in 2019 and did not survive the lunar night. *CNSA Chang'e-4.*
- The storm is the real one of 10 September 2017: X8.2 flare at 15:35 UT, particles 70 minutes later. *NASA DONKI; NOAA GOES-13.*
- 0.895 kg of oxygen per person per day. *NASA BVAD Rev 2 (2022).*
- A 10 kW solar array for the south pole. *NASA Langley, NASA/TM-20210011743.*
- About 20 cm of water as a storm shield. *NASA Langley: Townsend et al. 1988; Clowdsley 2021.*
- Real ground and sky. *LRO LOLA; JPL Horizons.*
- 29 sources ship inside the game: 21 NASA, 8 partner.

Problem and impact facts (checked 12 Nov 2026; say "about" and show the source in a corner):
- Space Camp, ages 9 to 11: **$1,799 for a week** in summer 2026. <https://spacecamp.com/groups/weeklong>
- Challenger Learning Centers: **33**, almost all in the United States. <https://challenger.org/our-impact/>
- **63%** of Bangladesh's school-age children have no internet at home. *UNICEF and ITU, 2020.*
- About **7.85 million** secondary-school students in Bangladesh in 2024. *BANBEIS, reported by Prothom Alo.*
- About **8,000** school digital labs by December 2021, with about **17 laptops** each. *Business Post; The Daily Star.*
- Our own sum: 17 laptops × 4 players = **68 students in one lesson**. If 1 lab in 8 runs one lesson a year with about 150 students, that is about **750,000 students in 5 years**. This is an estimate and must be labelled "estimate".

Playtest: 18 learners under 15 played the game. Use only real counts from the team's notes ("14 of 18 could explain…"). No percentages. No child's face, name or voice.
