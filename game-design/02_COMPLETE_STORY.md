## Before the story — Expedition Zero, 2034

Ferreira was a junior systems engineer. The biological trial was commissioned in 2034. Noor Rahman led that trial. Their early greenhouse was a set of seedling trays in a service annex, not a flowering garden. A fictional particle event damaged an exposed relay controller; an already degraded power connector made continued habitation unsafe. The expedition ordered retreat to the ascent vehicle; the young engineer Ferreira supported the evacuation. No one abandoned a crewmate.

Noor used her final authorised equipment-recovery minutes to put two dry mustard seeds in a powered storage drawer in the main habitat and twelve backup seeds in an insulated cold-trap vault reached by rover during an earlier survey. She did not make a last-minute impossible round trip. She left a map, her signed mission logs, and a pre-recorded care message on the horticulture recorder. Ferreira helped Noor board the ascent vehicle. De-energised seedling trays froze after evacuation. No plant flowered. Back in Bangladesh, Noor founded Last Light Academy, named after the last sunset she saw on the Moon, to train crews to finish the garden. Ferreira agreed to let the cadets discover the seed legacy: “Seeds you find mean more than seeds you’re given.” Safety diagrams remain available immediately; the mystery concerns personal history.

The service annex was sealed behind a damaged bulkhead. Reactivation surveys certified it isolated and nonessential; a warped hatch later becomes visible after a quake dislodges a floor cover. Discovery is of its contents and personal history, not an entire unmapped structure under a certified station.

## Chapter 1 — Night One

On 11 April 2046 the four cadets begin a field assignment across the coming polar season cycle: demonstrate that a small lunar greenhouse can reproduce plants across generations without sacrificing the habitat's safety. They have food supplies; one mustard flower cannot feed the crew or provide their oxygen. Ferreira's welcome is specific: operational decisions are theirs, emergency limits are not optional. Mei, the player's initial viewpoint, inventories an old dry-storage drawer and finds two seeds marked আলো and N. The team plants Alo in the external test chamber and Tara in the indoor reference bay. Kai checks the corridor light twice and jokes that it is an important scientific lamp. Rafi notices and leaves a low-power hand light within reach.

Two labelled weeks pass through inspection photos. The player returns to a familiar corridor now carrying small roots and handwritten names. On 25 April, two daylight work shifts let Amara store oxygen before the battery reaches capacity. The storm drill teaches shelter water and supply-bundle shielding. Rafi shares the historical replay warning about sunspot 2673. The science view labels the 2017 source separately from the expedition date. Everyone hears it; a specialist inbox is an information-distribution device, never an instruction to conceal danger.

At last light the Sun passes behind terrain. Earth's slow disappearance is compressed for presentation, with the radio countdown referring to predicted signal loss. Kai asks to keep decorative lights on. He admits he dislikes darkness; the others help him plan light rather than mock him. The player commits the remaining energy schedule and hears Ferreira's final link check. Local suit radios and the station network continue functioning.

The particle alarm sounds during the planned dark work shifts. The player chooses early water shielding, emergency pumping or stacking supply bundles. Life support has automatic safing; the moral problem is protecting the shared expedition, not choosing a child's death over a flower. After the storm the team drains the wall slowly enough to restore irrigation before Alo's stress exceeds its limit. At sunrise, a yellow flower opens. The player tags the bloom image, which triggers the recorder's old message: “If you're hearing this, Alo bloomed. Good. Now Alo needs a friend. — N.” It is an event-triggered care recording, not a prediction or supernatural computer. Days later, Earth contact returns. Ferreira says, “You didn't survive the Moon. You ran it.”

## Chapter 2 — Be the Bee

Tara's yellow flower is almost overlooked beside the celebrated Alo. Mei makes room for its name on the mission board. The player carries sealed pollen tools through the airlock and cross-pollinates the two compatible plants over seven dated flowering days with three reciprocal bee runs and seven care visits. Mei remembers her grandmother hand-pollinating orchard trees in China where pollinators were scarce; the game does not claim all bees disappeared. Rafi tries a bee impression over the radio, then stops when Amara asks him not to distract her counting.

An old recorder fragment identifies an earlier trial that stopped before seed production. To make the fragment legible, the player aligns timestamps and equipment labels rather than guesses a password. N. remembers her grandmother’s yellow mustard fields; a young engineer’s voice sounds like Ferreira. The players record a purple/green stem prediction for Chapter 4. The crew sends the evidence to Earth rather than secretly testing unsafe equipment. That evening, on 6 May, the floor trembles.

## Chapter 3 — Moonquake

Shaking dislodges a service cover and breaks an annex feedthrough. Amara's panel falsely suggests the main oxygen tank is at fault. The player uses an independent mechanical gauge, isolates the leaking garden spur, and applies a clamp from the safe side. Amara says she does not trust her numbers yet; Rafi answers that checking them is exactly her job. Plants remain on their local sealed atmosphere while the room circuit is isolated.

Ferreira supplies all relevant technical failure notes and repair diagrams immediately. Her personal involvement remains a suspicion until Chapter 5. Noor's name is not used to make a suspenseful safety gate. The isolated annex is made safe before entry. Below the exposed N.R. hatch lie frozen seedling trays and a map. Mei first mistakes them for flowering remains; the unopened buds and growth chart prove otherwise.

Time passes to 16 May, when Alo’s pods mature. A dated dry-down completes before the 21 May harvest. The player changes its care from growth to drying only after the monitor confirms maturity, then counts 42 seeds from the whole harvested plant. Mei asks if saving Alo meant it should live forever. Amara replies that their job was to let it finish. They save a pressed petal and seed lineage record. Twelve seeds are assigned to the next garden; thirty remain in the reserve. The player places the next seed in a tray. Discovery and grief turn into action.

## Chapter 4 — Into the Cold Trap

Five months later, on 21 October, a garden of twelve grows from those seeds. Rotation and cross-pollination over intervening Station Days are shown in a dated montage, not implied year-long survival of twelve original annual plants. Water-return planning reveals why the mapped cold-trap survey matters: the expedition needs a tested reserve source and the old biological backup. Rafi and Kai take the pressurised rover; Mei and Amara run the base. The base team sees the slope map while the field team sees the headlights, so they share observations by local radio.

The player rejects a short steep route for a longer surveyed ridge, checks return reserves, calibrates the sampling tool on dry ground and detects ice-bearing material. This is a sample and resource survey, not enough instantaneous extraction to supply the base. Headlamps reveal the insulated vault. The team lifts it with the closed carrier; recovered dry seeds are warmed sealed and quarantined at base. Twelve distinct seed specimens are inventoried, including cotton; their exact collection mapping is in the inventory ledger. The collection album is separate from biological seed counts. The lid says, “If you found this, you're ready. Ask Commander Ferreira about Expedition Zero.” Ferreira falls quiet. Rao says, “She… needs a minute.” The inscription sets up her Chapter 5 confession; it does not disclose Noor’s identity early.

## Chapter 5 — Shooting Stars

Ferreira arrives physically on 12 December on a planned relief flight carrying replacement hardware. Her first act is to take a corridor watch while the cadets eat. On 14 December the crew observes amplified instrument images of Geminid impact flashes, not atmospheric streaks overhead. The player aligns event timestamps to reconstruct the 2034 relay failure and overlays the hatch log, vault manifest and evacuation record.

Ferreira confesses her role as Expedition Zero’s youngest engineer and the radio she could not repair: “Noor stayed to the last minute to hide the seeds. I was the engineer who couldn’t fix the radio. We left her garden to freeze. I left it.” The recovered manifest identifies Dr. Noor Rahman, a Bangladeshi botanist. Rao, with her permission, says, “Noor is alive. She has read every one of your reports.” Noor founded their Academy after returning to Bangladesh. Rao has been forwarding their reports to her all along. The crew chooses a respectful response; all dialogue paths invite rather than demand her involvement. They send a short report carrying the sound of twelve healthy plants' fans. Noor acknowledges in text, not yet a voice call. A cotton seed is planted as an indoor comparison specimen.

## Chapter 6 — The Red Ring

On 12 January 2047 the crew uses an external camera to observe the Earth occulting the Sun during the real total lunar eclipse date. Aurora's precise naked-eye view is not claimed verified. The monitor carries the red rim; local observation may be partly terrain-obstructed. “Every sunrise and sunset” is poetic shorthand for sunlight refracted through Earth's atmospheric limb. Bangladesh's place is shown on a conceptual Earth diagram, not presented as a resolved geographical stripe in the ring.

Kai builds the eclipse power plan himself. He tells the player he is still afraid but can work with it. When a short feeder interruption occurs, he uses the hand light, calls the team and restores the correct bus. The payoff is action with support, not a magical cure. A message from N says, “I watched a red ring from there once.” Now everyone knows who N is; her signature is affection, not renewed secrecy. A large active-region alert then prompts increased monitoring, with no assertion that a sunspot forecasts an exact storm months ahead.

## Chapter 7 — The Great Storm

Routine observations continue until the replay warning on 14 February 2047. The chapter opens with “What if? Historical 2012 storm replay.” It adapts STEREO-A’s July 2012 measurements to a fictional Moon strike; the three playable waves and shelter scores are game abstractions, not measured lunar doses. Ferreira gives local command to the cadets: “Your call, cadets.” They isolate electronics, keep minimum services running and protect the original thirty-seed founder reserve inside the shelter. Earlier repairs and shared observations now serve one integrated plan.

In a lull, Noor's final log plays: “We're leaving at last light. I'm calling the seeds Alo, light, so whoever finds them brings the light back.” Her final log also explains that the Academy takes its name from that last light. The controller fails despite isolation; a damaged external feedthrough, not a mysterious magic pulse, disables the direct uplink. Local radios survive. The crew waits for the radiation monitor to clear the route before any EVA. Earth sets behind Aurora's terrain on the fictional mission schedule. The backup legacy relay can bypass the damaged base feedthrough if it is repaired at Malapert. Ferreira knows its hardware and joins the repair expedition. A dated February-to-April preparation montage shows stable local operations, route surveys, relay fabrication and repeated garden care. The blackout does not leave the crew in an unaddressed life-support emergency during the interval.

## Chapter 8 — The Lighthouse

At 00:00 on 8 April, Ferreira, Kai and Rafi leave in the pressurised rover. Mei and Amara remain at Aurora. A preplanned 120-km route takes approximately 24 hours with rests; playable segments compress cruise. At Aurora's sunset, the two homebound cadets activate the garden's proven overnight loop. The player switches between the teams through authored scene boundaries, never by physically teleporting a cadet.

At Malapert at 00:00 on 9 April, the three replace the damaged controller and align the dish using both an Earth view and a local link to Aurora. A bright summit does not automatically see the station; the final relay is an upgraded two-link installation with surveyed repeater buoys on the route. As Earth reaches Aurora’s terrain horizon, the player closes both relay links. The deadline is a planned handover window, with an accessible untimed equivalent. Noor's voice arrives: “Hello, Aurora. This is Noor.” On a rooftop in Bangladesh she looks at a Moon represented by a fictional local visibility shot; no precise lunar phase claim is made. “You did what I couldn't.” Ferreira answers that Noor brought them the seeds. Mei makes the connection: “Alo means light. So does Noor.” The garden belongs to everyone who kept it alive.

The rover returns by 03:00 on 10 April after repairs and a compressed 24-hour return. Three hours later, first light reaches Aurora. The player opens a viewport shutter only after the thermal check, sees the established mustard garden flowering and the cotton plant alive beside it. The garden has survived the next seasonal darkness while maintaining seed reserves and crew safety. Alo's pressed petal remains beside its descendants. Ferreira says, “You didn't survive the Moon. You grew it.”

## Epilogue and ending contract

The player writes the shared expedition record by selecting one of three already-authored dedication lines, all equally valid. The final photo names all four cadets, Ferreira and Noor. A quiet walk to the first drawer completes the emotional circle. Credits show the station growing familiar, not an advertisement for an unfinished ending. After credits the player may open an unlocked document drawer labelled MARS; no Mars mission is playable or required. All central questions are answered before this tease. Continue loads the safe final garden, with chapter replay separately available and clearly identified as replay outside campaign chronology.
